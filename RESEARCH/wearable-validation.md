# Wearable Validation

Consumer wearable sleep metrics are useful but imperfect proxies for laboratory sleep measurement.

A meta-analysis of 24 studies involving 798 participants found significant differences between consumer wearables and PSG in multiple sleep parameters.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39484805/

A recent comparative study including Garmin devices found high sensitivity for detecting sleep but lower specificity for wake and low/moderate agreement for sleep stages.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40303381/

A broader wearable validation review also supports caution around stage accuracy.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38557808/

A 2026 review reports only low-to-moderate concordance between consumer wearable measures and subjective sleep quality.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41946254/

## Product consequence
Never convert a Garmin estimate directly into a clinical assertion.

Represent:
- source;
- quality;
- confidence;
- missingness;
- uncertainty.

Prefer trends and within-person changes over absolute stage values.
